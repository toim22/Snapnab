Snapnab User Manual

Everything Snapnab can do, explained step by step: capturing images, recording video, annotating and editing, and keeping your captures organized.

Snapnab is a screen capture, video recording and image editing tool for Windows. It is made of two programs that work together:

Snapnab (the capturer)

Lives in the notification area next to the clock. It owns the global shortcuts, the capture window, the crosshair, the video recorder and One Click.

Snapnab Editor

Opens your captures so you can annotate, highlight, blur, crop, apply effects, recognize text, cut videos, make GIFs and save or share the result.

The Library

Every capture and recording is kept automatically in a library folder, so nothing is lost even if you close the Editor without saving.

How to read this manual

  • New to Snapnab? Read Installing and starting and do the five quick-start lessons. They take about fifteen minutes and cover what most people use every day.
  • Each later chapter is a complete reference for one area. You do not have to read them in order.
  • Use the search box at the top of the contents to find any word in the manual. Press Esc in the box to clear it.
  • Press F1 in the capture window or in the Editor to open this manual. In the Editor, F1 jumps to the description of the tool you are using.

In the text, names in bold are labels you see on screen, a path like File › Options… means "open the File menu and choose Options…", and keys look like Ctrl+S.

Your captures stay on your computer.Snapnab does not send anything to the internet: there is no account, no cloud upload and no telemetry.

Installing and starting

Snapnab runs on Windows 10 version 2004 or later, or Windows 11 (64-bit). Nothing else needs to be installed.

Installer or portable

PackageWhat to do
Snapnab-<version>-setup.exeRun it and follow the wizard. Choose to install just for you (no administrator prompt) or for all users. The wizard creates the Start menu shortcuts and can add a desktop shortcut and start Snapnab with Windows. If Snapnab is running, the installer closes it before updating. To uninstall, use Windows Settings › Apps; your captures and settings are kept.
Snapnab-<version>-win-x64.zipPortable version. Extract it to any folder and run Snapnab.exe. Keep the lang and help folders next to the programs.

What you see after starting

  1. The Snapnab icon appears in the notification area (next to the clock). If you do not see it, click the small arrow that shows hidden icons; you can drag the icon onto the taskbar to keep it visible.
  2. The capture window opens. You can close it: Snapnab keeps running in the notification area and the shortcuts keep working. To stop it showing at every start, turn off Show the capture window at startup in File › Options….
  3. Press Print Screen at any time to capture.

Quick start: 5 lessons

Each lesson takes two or three minutes. Do them in order; each one builds on the previous one.

Lesson 1 — Capture part of the screen

  1. Press Print Screen. The screen freezes and a crosshair appears, with a magnifier and the pixel coordinates next to the pointer.
  2. Drag a rectangle around what you want. The size in pixels is shown above the rectangle. Release the mouse button.
  3. The capture opens in Snapnab Editor. It is already saved in your Library.
Shortcut inside the crosshairInstead of dragging, move the pointer over a window, a button or a panel: Snapnab outlines it. A single click captures exactly that area. Enter captures the whole monitor and Esc cancels.

Lesson 2 — Annotate it

  1. In the Editor, press A (Arrow) and drag from where the arrow starts to where it points.
  2. Right after drawing, just type: the text appears at the tail of the arrow. Click elsewhere to finish.
  3. Press H (Highlighter) and drag over a line of text to highlight it.
  4. Press N (Steps) and click three times on the image: you get the numbers 1, 2 and 3.
  5. Press B (Blur) and drag over something private, such as an e-mail address.
  6. Made a mistake? Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Y redoes. Everything you drew stays editable: press V (Move), click an object and drag it, or change its color in the Properties panel on the right.

Lesson 3 — Share it

  1. Press Ctrl+Shift+C (Copy all) and paste the annotated image into a chat, an e-mail or a document with Ctrl+V.
  2. Or press Ctrl+Shift+S (Save as) to save a PNG, JPG, PDF or another format.
  3. Or drag the capture's thumbnail from the Recent captures strip at the bottom of the Editor straight into another program.

Lesson 4 — Record a short video

  1. Press Shift+Print Screen and drag the area to record (or click a window, or press Enter for the whole monitor).
  2. A red frame marks the area and a small recording panel appears. Check that the microphone and system audio buttons are as you want, then click the red record button.
  3. After a 3-second countdown, recording starts. Shift+F9 pauses and resumes; Shift+F10 (or Shift+Print Screen again) stops.
  4. The MP4 opens in the Editor, where you can play it, cut out parts and make a GIF.

Lesson 5 — Make it one keystroke

  1. Open the capture window (double-click the notification area icon).
  2. Set it up the way you like, for example Selection: Window, Effects: Shadow, Copy to clipboard on.
  3. Click + at the bottom and choose New preset from current settings. Give it a name.
  4. In the preset list, click Add shortcut on its row and press a key combination, for example Ctrl+Shift+1. That key now runs the whole setup from anywhere in Windows.

You now know the core workflow. The rest of this manual explains every option in detail.

The crosshair and selecting

For most selection types, Snapnab first freezes the screen and shows a crosshair. Because the image is frozen, tooltips, hover effects and animations stay exactly as they were when you pressed the key.

  • Magnifier — a zoomed view of the pixels around the pointer, with the screen coordinates (X, Y) and the color under the pointer (swatch and hex code). Use it to start a selection on the exact pixel.
  • Size label — while you drag, the width × height of the selection is shown above it.
  • Hint bar — a strip at the top of the monitor reminds you of the keys for the current mode.
Key / mouseEffect
DragSelects a rectangle (Region mode).
ClickCaptures the highlighted window, control or area under the pointer.
EnterCaptures the whole monitor under the pointer (Region mode); finishes a multiple-area selection.
Arrow keysMove the pointer by 1 pixel (with Shift, by 10 pixels) for pixel-perfect selections.
BackspaceRemoves the last area in Multiple areas.
Right clickFinishes Multiple areas; cancels the other modes.
EscCancels the capture.
Need to open a menu or hover something first?Set a Delay in the capture window. The countdown ("Capturing in 3 s…") gives you time to open the menu or tooltip before the screen freezes.

Region and fixed region

Region combines the most common selections in one mode: drag a rectangle, click a highlighted window or control, or press Enter for the whole monitor.

The gear ⚙ in the Selection box offers two ways to choose the region:

  • Select the region at capture time — the normal, interactive crosshair.
  • Fixed region… — always the same size. Enter the Width (px) and Height (px). During the capture a rectangle of that size follows the pointer; click to capture. Turn on Always capture at the same position and enter Left (X) and Top (Y) to skip the crosshair entirely: the capture happens instantly at that spot. The box then shows "Fixed region".
When to use a fixed regionDocumentation where every screenshot must be the same size, product images for a store, or repeating a capture of the same dashboard every day.

Window, Full screen and Current monitor

  • Window — the pointer becomes a hand; click the window to capture. In video, the recording follows the window's outline at the moment you click.
  • Full screen (all monitors) — captures every monitor at once, with no crosshair.
  • Current monitor — captures only the monitor where the mouse pointer is.

For Full screen and Current monitor, the gear offers Each window as a separate object.

Freehand, Multiple areas and Object

  • Freehand — hold the mouse button and draw an outline around what you want. When you release, the area inside the outline is captured and everything outside it is transparent.
  • Multiple areas — drag as many rectangles as you want. Backspace removes the last one. Press Enter or right-click to finish. The result is one image containing all the areas in their original positions; the space between them is transparent.
  • Object (control under the cursor) — move the pointer over a button, a toolbar, a list or any other element; it is outlined. Click to capture just that element.

Panoramic scrolling

Captures content that is longer or wider than the screen while you scroll it. It works in almost any program: web browsers, chat apps, spreadsheets, PDF viewers, code editors, wide timelines.

  1. Choose the Panoramic scrolling capture preset (or set Selection: Panoramic scrolling) and start the capture.
  2. Drag a rectangle around the part of the window that scrolls (the page content, not the whole window).
  3. A frame and a small bar appear: "Scroll the content inside the frame…". Scroll at a normal pace with the mouse wheel, the scroll bar or the keyboard, down or sideways. The bar shows the size captured so far.
  4. Click Finish. The frames are stitched into one long image. Cancel discards it.
Tips for clean resultsOnly include the scrolling part in the frame. Sticky headers outside the frame are not repeated. Scroll steadily; scrolling back up a little is fine. Very long images are limited to 32,000 pixels.

Automatic scrolling (window)

Snapnab scrolls the window for you and stitches the result. The built-in preset Scrolling (full page) uses it.

  1. Start the capture; the pointer becomes a hand.
  2. Click inside the scrollable area (for example, a web page).
  3. Snapnab scrolls it from top to bottom with the mouse wheel, detects how far each step moved and joins the pieces. Do not move the mouse until it finishes.

If the program scrolls in an unusual way (animated scrolling, lazy-loading pages), use Panoramic scrolling instead.

Extended window

Some windows can show more content if they are bigger than the screen. Extended window temporarily enlarges a window, captures it and puts it back.

  1. Start the capture and click the window.
  2. In the Extended window dialog, enter the New width (px) and New height (px) (up to 19,500 pixels).
  3. The window is resized, captured and restored to its original size and position.

Menu, Clipboard and Graphics file

  • Menu — captures the open menu and its open submenus together. Use a Delay so you have time to open the menu first. If no open menu is found, Snapnab lets you select a region instead.
  • Clipboard — uses the image currently on the clipboard as the capture, so it gets your preset's effects and destinations and goes to the Library. Also available in Capture › From the clipboard.
  • Graphics file — asks for an image file and treats it as a capture. Also in Capture › From a graphics file….

Each window as a separate object, and page links

The Selection gear has two more options for image presets:

Each window as a separate object

Available for Full screen and Current monitor. The capture opens in the Editor with the desktop wallpaper and the taskbar as the background and every visible window as its own image object, named after its title. You can then move windows around, hide or delete one (for example a private chat), or rearrange them for a clean screenshot. Each window is captured complete, even the parts that were covered by other windows. Use the Layers panel to see and manage them.

Keep the page links (clickable, also in PDF)

On by default. When you capture a web browser or document viewer, Snapnab records where the links are and their addresses. In the Editor, resting the mouse on a link area shows its address; double-click opens it. When you export to PDF, the links stay clickable. Links are kept when the capture effects only resize or add a border; effects that change the geometry (rotate, trim, edges, shadow, capture info) drop them.

Capture effects

Capture effects are applied automatically to every capture made with the preset, before it reaches the Editor or any destination. They are perfect for a consistent look: every screenshot with the same border and shadow, a watermark on everything, or captures always resized to 50%.

Adding, changing and removing effects

  1. Open the Effects box and choose an effect. Its settings open; click OK to turn it on.
  2. To combine effects, open the box again and choose Add another effect.
  3. To change one, choose it again in the list. To turn one off, choose it and then Remove this effect. None turns them all off.

Capturing text (OCR)

Use it to copy text that cannot be selected: an error message, a video frame, a scanned document, text in an image.

  1. Run the built-in Grab Text preset (or any preset with Share: Text (OCR)).
  2. Select the area with the text.
  3. The recognized text is copied to the clipboard and shown in a window, where you can check it or copy part of it.

Text recognition uses the OCR engine built into Windows, so the languages it reads are the ones installed in Windows Settings › Time & language › Language. If no OCR language is installed, Snapnab tells you so. You can also recognize text of an image already open in the Editor: see Grab Text in the Editor.

Better recognitionCapture at 100% zoom or larger, avoid busy backgrounds, and include whole lines.

One Click

One Click is a small, discreet panel docked to an edge of the screen. It stays collapsed as a thin colored strip and opens when the mouse moves over it.

  • Turn it on or off with View › Show One Click in the capture window, or from the notification area menu.
  • The red button starts an image capture with the current preset.
  • The gear opens a menu: Capture image, Record video, Presets, Open the capture window and Hide One Click.
  • Drag the panel to move it; when you let go, it snaps to the nearest edge of the nearest monitor. Its position is remembered.
  • One Click hides itself during captures and never appears in your images or videos.

Part 2 — Recording video

Setting up a recording

Snapnab records the screen as an MP4 video (H.264 video and AAC audio), with microphone and system audio. Open the Video tab of the capture window to set it up.

Recording, step by step

  1. Press Shift+Print Screen (or the record button on the Video tab).
  2. Select the area: drag a rectangle, click a window or press Enter for the monitor. The hint bar starts with "Recording:" so you know you are selecting for a video.
  3. A red frame marks the recorded area and the recording panel appears below it (or above it, or inside it if there is no room). The panel says "Ready". The audio meters already move, so you can check your microphone before starting.
  4. Click the red record button. A 3-second countdown appears; click Skip (or press the record button or shortcut again) to start right away.
  5. Record. The panel shows the elapsed time.
  6. Click stop or press Shift+F10. The MP4 is saved to the Library and opens in the Editor.
Not in your videoThe red frame, the recording panel, the volume panel and One Click are invisible to the recording. You can leave them where they are.

Disk space and safety

  • When less than 1 GB is free on the Library's drive, Snapnab warns you. If the disk fills up, the recording stops and what was recorded is saved.
  • The computer does not go to sleep while recording.
  • If you try to exit Snapnab during a recording, it asks before stopping.

Microphone and system audio

The microphone and the system audio are recorded on separate channels and mixed into the video's sound track.

  • Volume panel — click the sliders button on the recording panel. Each source has a slider from 0% to 200% (it sticks at 100%) and a live level meter that shows the level after the volume, so you can see whether you are too quiet or too loud.
  • Automatic microphone gain — evens out the microphone level, raising a quiet voice and avoiding clipping. Turn it off if you have a good microphone and set the level by hand.
  • The volumes work before and during the recording, and are saved back into the preset, so next time you start with the same values. The defaults can also be set in the Quality gear (Video options).
Check before you recordThe meters move as soon as the recording panel appears, before you press record. Say a few words and watch the microphone meter.

Webcam

You can show your face in a corner of the video, as in a presentation or tutorial.

  1. On the Video tab, open the Webcam menu and choose your camera.
  2. In the same menu, choose the Shape: Circle, Rounded square, Square, Rectangle 16:9 or Rectangle 4:3.
  3. Start the recording. The webcam appears inside the recorded area, mirrored like a mirror. Drag it to where you want it, even during the recording.
  4. Use the webcam button on the recording panel to hide it and show it again at any time.

Removing the background (chroma key)

Remove background (chroma key)… in the Webcam menu removes a plain background behind you, so only you appear in the video, without a frame.

  • Background color — the color of your background (green is ideal).
  • Tolerance (0–100) — how different a color can be and still be removed. Raise it if bits of background remain; lower it if parts of you disappear.
  • Use a plain, evenly lit background. A green screen gives the best result.

If Windows blocks the camera, allow it in Windows Settings › Privacy & security › Camera.

Part 3 — Presets and shortcuts

A preset stores everything the capture window shows: the type (image or video), the selection, the effects, the destination, the switches, the delay, the recording options and the webcam. Presets let you switch between completely different setups with one click or one key.

Using the preset list

Click ⚙ Presets at the bottom of the capture window (or View › Presets list) to show the list.

  • Click a preset to load it into the window. Any change you make is saved into that preset.
  • Double-click a preset to capture with it.
  • Add shortcut on a row gives the preset its own global shortcut.
  • The gear on a row: Capture with this preset, Rename…, Shortcut…, Duplicate and Delete. At least one preset must remain.

Creating presets

The + button next to Presets offers:

  • New preset from current settings — saves what the window shows now as a new preset.
  • New preset… — starts a new preset with the default settings.
  • Help — a short reminder of how presets work.
A good habitKeep the everyday preset selected, and create presets for special jobs, each with a shortcut: "Window with shadow to clipboard", "Product photo 800×800 to file", "Tutorial video with webcam"…

Sharing presets

File › Export presets… saves all presets to a .snappresets file; File › Import presets… adds the presets from such a file. To move all settings to another computer, see Backing up your settings.

Global shortcuts

Global shortcuts work anywhere in Windows, even when Snapnab is hidden. Change them in File › Options…:

  • If another program already uses it, Snapnab warns you and intercepts it with a keyboard hook, so the key still works for Snapnab.

Part 4 — The Editor

Zoom and view

  • Ctrl+mouse wheel zooms around the pointer (smoothly on touchpads). Ctrl++ / Ctrl+- zoom in and out, Ctrl+1 shows the actual size, Ctrl+0 fits the image to the window.
  • Scroll with the mouse wheel and the scroll bars, or drag with the middle mouse button.
  • View › Background color of the area… changes the color around the image.
  • View also shows or hides the Effects panel, Layers panel, Library, Recent captures (tray) and Rulers and guides.

Objects: the basics

Everything you draw in the Editor — arrows, text, shapes, steps, stamps, blurs — is an object that floats above the image. Objects stay editable until you merge them into the image, and they are kept editable in the .snap file.

Drawing

  • Choose a tool on the toolbar or with its letter key (tool reference), then drag on the canvas.
  • A drawing tool only picks up objects of its own kind; over other objects it draws. So you can highlight a text or point an arrow at a stamp without moving them. Hold Ctrl to draw over an object of the same kind.
  • Shift while drawing makes perfect squares, circles and 45° lines. See Perfect geometry.

Selecting, moving and resizing

  • With Move (V), click an object to select it. Handles appear: drag a corner or side to resize, drag the object to move it. Yellow handles adjust special points (a callout's tail, a magnifier's lens, a curved arrow's bend).
  • Arrow keys nudge the selected object by 1 pixel; Shift+arrow by 10 pixels.
  • Shift while dragging an existing object locks the movement to the first direction you moved.
  • Double-click a text, callout or arrow label to edit its text.
  • Del deletes the selection. Esc cancels the current action.

Selecting several objects

  • Ctrl+click or Shift+click adds or removes an object from the selection.
  • With Move, drag on an empty part of the image to draw a selection rectangle: every object it touches is selected.
  • Ctrl+A selects all objects.
  • Selected objects move, delete, change order, align, flip and merge together. A style change applies to the selected objects of the same kind as the last one clicked.

Changing an object's look

Select it and use the Properties tab: click a quick style, or change colors, width, font and the other properties. Changes apply live. The tool also remembers your last settings for the next object, even after restarting.

Right-click menu

Right-click the canvas for: cut, copy, paste, paste in place, delete, lock/unlock, rename, order, align, distribute, group, drop the floating piece and open link.

Undo

Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes, and Edit › Undo all returns to the original capture. Every capture has its own undo history.

Quick styles, themes and favorites

At the top of the Properties tab, every tool shows a gallery of quick styles — ready-made combinations of colors, widths, fonts and shadows. Click one to use it for the next object, or to restyle the selected object.

  • + saves the current settings as a new quick style.
  • Star on a style adds it to your favorites. Right-click a style for Apply this style, Add to favorites / Remove from favorites and Delete this quick style.
  • The Favorites tool (star icon on the toolbar) shows your favorite styles from all tools in one place. Picking one switches to the right tool automatically.

Themes

The quick styles of all tools are generated from a theme of up to four colors. Choose the theme in the Theme: box (Basic, Industrial or your own). Its gear offers New theme…, Edit theme… (or Edit a copy of the theme… for built-in ones), duplicate, Import theme, Export theme (.snaptheme file, to share with colleagues) and delete. Use your company colors in a theme and every tool matches your brand.

Annotation tools

Move (V)

Selects, moves and resizes objects. Click an object to select it; Ctrl+click or drag a rectangle on the background to select several. With the Smart Move switch on, you can also drag an area of the image itself — for example a button — and move it somewhere else; the place it left is filled in automatically to match the surroundings.

Arrow (A)

Drag from the tail to the head. Shift keeps the angle in 45° steps. Properties: color, shadow, start and end caps (8 arrowhead kinds), dashes, width, opacity, start and end size, and Bézier curve — drag the middle handle to bend the arrow.

Text on the arrow: right after drawing an arrow, simply type. The text appears beyond the tail and follows the arrow when either end moves. Double-click it to edit later; Text font and Text size set its look. (While an arrow is just drawn, letter keys type the label instead of switching tools; click elsewhere or press Esc first.)

Line (L)

Like the arrow, without heads by default. Shift keeps 45° steps. Supports caps, dashes and Bézier curves.

Shape (R)

Rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse, triangle, diamond, parallelogram, hexagon and star. Properties: Fill (a color, a gradient or no color for an empty frame), Outline, Shape, shadow, thickness and opacity. Shift makes it perfect (square, circle, equilateral triangle, regular hexagon).

Pen (P)

Draws freehand lines: circle something, underline, sign. Properties: color, shadow, dashes, width and opacity.

Text (T)

Click to start typing, or drag to create a box of a fixed width; the text wraps and the box grows downward. Double-click edits. Properties: fill (text color), outline (a contour around the letters for contrast), shadow, font, size, outline thickness, and under Advanced bold/italic/underline, alignment, opacity, padding and Line spacing (%) (80% to 300%).

Callout (C)

A text box with a shape: 24 shapes, including speech bubbles, clouds, thought bubbles, boxes with arrows, notes, explosions and whispers. Drag to create it and type. The yellow handle moves the tail (or the direction of arrow shapes). Properties: fill, outline, shape, shadow, font and text color, size, thickness, arrow style/color/size for shapes with a pointer, and:

  • Blur the background (frosted glass) — blurs what is behind the callout, with an adjustable amount; combine with Fill opacity for a modern translucent look.
  • Shaded (gradient) under Advanced, plus the same text options as the Text tool.

Steps (N)

Click to place numbered markers in sequence — perfect for "click here, then here" instructions. Properties: fill, text color, shape (circle, teardrop or square), shadow, opacity, Type (1, 2, 3… / A, B, C… / a, b, c… / I, II, III… / i, ii, iii…), Restart the sequence (next step is 1 again), and an Outline with its width for contrast on any background.

Stamp (K)

Places a color emoji, a symbol, a label or your own image. See Stamps.

Highlight and hide tools

Highlighter (H)

Drag over what you want to highlight; it is tinted with the chosen color and opacity, like a highlighter pen. Choose the area shape: rectangle, ellipse, polygon, freehand and more.

Blur (B)

Hides sensitive information: passwords, e-mails, faces, account numbers. Drag over the area. Type: Blur or Pixelate; Intensity; and the area shape. The blur stays an editable object: you can move or resize it, and it updates with what is underneath.

Before sharingFor truly sensitive data, use a high intensity, or cover it with a solid Shape. Light blurs on short text can sometimes be guessed.

Spotlight (G)

Drag over the area to focus on; everything else is darkened. Intensity sets how dark; the area can have any shape. Add several spotlights to focus on more than one area.

Magnify (Z)

Shows a zoomed-in view of a detail. Drag to create the magnifier. Properties: outline, shape (round, square, hexagon, diamond, star), shadow, Magnification (%) (110% to 800%), thickness, and:

  • Lens separate from the magnified area — the lens can sit anywhere; the yellow handle chooses the area it magnifies.
  • Realistic magnifier (rim and reflection) — a thick shaded rim with reflection; With handle adds a handle.

Image tools

These tools change the pixels of the image itself (they are not objects).

Selection (S)

Selects part of the image to move, copy, delete or crop. Mode: Rectangle, Ellipse, Lasso (freehand) or Magic wand. Then:

  • Drag the selection to move it (Smart Move); Fill when moving decides what is left behind: Transparency, Canvas color (white) or Smart fill (rebuilds the background).
  • Del erases the selection; Ctrl+C copies it.
  • Buttons: Crop to selection, Copy selection, Delete selection, Lift out the selection (floating piece).
  • The selection snaps to contrast edges such as window borders and table lines (Edit › Selection snaps to image edges; hold Alt to turn it off for a moment). Shift makes a perfect square or circle.

Magic Wand

Selects by color: click an area and every connected pixel of a similar color is selected. Tolerance decides how similar; Global selection (same color) selects that color everywhere in the image. It is the Selection tool in Magic wand mode, so the same move, fill and crop options apply.

Crop (X)

Drag the area to keep; the size is shown as you drag. Enter or double-click applies, Esc cancels. The crop area snaps to contrast edges and can be typed exactly in Crop area (px). Buttons: Apply cut and Auto trim (removes uniform borders).

Cut Out (U)

Removes a strip from the middle of the image and joins the parts — great for shortening a long screenshot. Direction: Horizontal (removes rows) or Vertical (removes columns). Drag over the strip to remove.

Fill (F)

Click to fill an area of similar color with the fill color or a gradient. Tolerance sets how similar the colors must be; Same color across the image fills every occurrence; the eyedropper button picks the fill color from the image.

Eraser (E)

Drag to erase image pixels; they become transparent. Width, Opacity (partial erasing) and Smooth edges.

Eyedropper (I)

Click the image to pick a color. It is applied to the drawing tools, so your next arrow or text matches the color you picked.

Favorites

Shows your favorite quick styles from every tool. Choose one to draw with it.

Area shapes and perfect geometry

Area shapes

Highlighter, Blur and Spotlight can use any of these shapes (the Shape swatch in their properties): rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse/circle, triangle, diamond, parallelogram, hexagon, star, polygon and freehand.

  • Polygon — click each corner. To close it, click the first corner, double-click, or press Enter. Backspace removes the last corner; Esc cancels.
  • Freehand — drag around the area.
  • You can change the shape of an existing area later; a polygon or freehand outline then starts from its box.

Colors and gradients

Clicking any color swatch opens the palette:

  • Theme colors (from the current theme), Standard colors and Recent colors.
  • No color — transparent (for example a shape with no fill).
  • More colors… — any color, with a picker.
  • Gradients — ready-made gradient presets, and Edit gradient… to make your own.

Gradients

Gradients work in the Shape fill, the Fill tool and Image › Canvas color…. In Edit gradient…:

  • Type: Linear or Radial (from the center).
  • Angle (degrees) for linear gradients: 0° goes left to right, 90° top to bottom.
  • Start color, End color and up to two middle colors, each with a Position (%).

The Layers panel

The Layers tab (third tab on the right, or View › Layers panel) lists every object from the front to the back, with a thumbnail. The image itself is the Background (image) row at the bottom.

  • Eye — hides or shows an object. Hidden objects are not drawn and not exported.
  • Lock — locks or unlocks it.
  • Name — double-click or press F2 to rename (for example "Login button arrow").
  • Groups expand to show the objects inside.
  • Select — click, Ctrl+click and Shift+click select objects; the selection is shared with the canvas.
  • Reorder — drag rows up or down.
  • Right-click for the object menu.

Visibility, locks and names are saved in the .snap file. The Layers panel is especially useful with windows as objects captures.

Rulers, guides and exact sizes

Rulers and guides

View › Rulers and guides (Ctrl+Shift+R) shows rulers that follow the zoom and scrolling, and the pointer's X/Y position in the status bar.

  • Drag from a ruler onto the image to create a guide.
  • Move a guide with the Move tool; drag it back onto the ruler to remove it. View › Remove the guides removes all.
  • With Canvas snapping on, objects snap to the guides. Guides are saved with the capture.

Exact position and size

When an object, a selection or a crop area is active, the Properties panel shows X, Y, W and H in image pixels. Type a value and it applies live. The chain button (Keep proportions) keeps the aspect ratio. Example: choose Crop and type W 800 and H 600 to crop to exactly 800×600.

Pasting text into a text or callout

By default (Edit › Paste text without formatting), pasted text takes the object's style. While typing, Ctrl+Shift+V does the opposite and keeps the source font, size, color and bold/italic/underline.

Editing the image itself

Lift a piece, edit the rest, drop it back

Useful when you want to crop or cut out around something but keep that thing.

  1. Select an area with the Selection tool.
  2. Edit › Lift out the selection (floating piece) (Ctrl+Shift+L). The area becomes a floating piece.
  3. Crop, cut out or edit the rest of the image. Move the piece where you want it.
  4. Press Enter, double-click the piece, or choose Drop the piece into the image to merge it.

  1. Open the Effects tab. At the top are the quick effects; below them, every effect with a preview of your image.
  2. Click an effect. Its settings open in the panel and the canvas shows the result live as you change them.
  3. Click Apply to keep it (one Ctrl+Z undoes it) or Cancel.

Quick effects

Quick effects apply a combination in one click: Shadow, Border and shadow, Torn paper, Page curl, Wave, Sawtooth (Jagged), Fade, Bevel… To make your own, set up the border, edges, page curl and shadow in the panel and click Add (Add to quick effects). Right-click one of yours to delete it.

Stamps

The Stamp tool (K) places colorful emoji, symbols and labels — or your own images.

  • Categories: Pinned, Labels, Business, Interface, Activities, Food & drink, Holidays, Smileys & people, Stickers, Symbols, Travel & places and My stamps.
  • Labels are badges with text in your interface language: Before/After, Wrong/Right, Incorrect/Correct, New, Updated, Yes/No, Do/Don't, Important, Tip, Note, Example, Draft, Beta, Deprecated and Coming soon.
  • Search stamps by name (for example "check", "warning", "rocket").
  • Click on the image to place a stamp at its default size, or drag to choose the size (Shift keeps its proportions).
  • Import… adds your own images (logos, icons, signatures) to My stamps.
  • Organize: drag stamps in the gallery to reorder them; right-click › Pin to the top to keep favorites first and in the Pinned category. The order is remembered.

Grab Text in the Editor

Edit › Grab Text (OCR) (Ctrl+Shift+O) recognizes the text in the current image and shows it in a window, from where you can copy it. It uses the same Windows OCR as Capturing text.

Customizing the toolbar

By default the toolbar is automatic: Move, Selection, Text, Highlighter and Shape first, then your most recently used tools, and Magnify at the end. More lists every tool with its icon, name and shortcut.

To make it your own, choose More › Customize toolbar… (or View › Customize toolbar…):

  • The window lists Drawing tools, Menu items, Sharing destinations, Effects and Miscellaneous (spacer).
  • Drag an item onto the toolbar; the toolbar shows where it will go.
  • On the toolbar, drag an item to move it, or drag it off the toolbar to remove it.
  • Number of tools on the automatic toolbar chooses 6 to 16.
  • Restore defaults goes back to the automatic toolbar; Done keeps your changes.

Besides tools, you can add effects, outputs (file, copy all, e-mail, print, external program), commands (undo, redo, new image, open, save, duplicate, delete, merge all, rotate, trim, Grab Text, align, distribute, lock, rulers) and spacers. More › Back to default (automatic) undoes the customization.

Recent captures and the Library

The Library

Every capture and recording is saved automatically in the Library. Images are stored as .snap files, which keep all objects editable; videos as MP4. You never lose a capture by forgetting to save it.

Recent captures tray

The strip at the bottom of the Editor shows your most recent captures (10 by default). New captures and opened files go to the front. Hide or show it with View › Recent captures (tray).

  • Click a thumbnail to open it. Rest the mouse on it for a larger preview with its annotations, size and date (videos show the first frame and the duration).
  • Select several with Ctrl+click, Shift+click, Ctrl+A, or by dragging a rectangle from empty space (as in File Explorer).
  • Drag thumbnails into other programs — chats, e-mails, documents, File Explorer. Images go as PNG with the annotations; videos as MP4.
  • Drop files from File Explorer onto the Editor to import them.
  • Right-click: Open, Show in folder, Export…, Select all, Rename… (F2), Remove from tray (it stays in the Library) and Delete (to the Recycle Bin, Del).

The Library view

Click Library on the tray (or View › Library) to see all captures in a grid in place of the image; Back to editor returns.

  • Search by name filters by capture name.
  • Multiple selection as in the tray. With items selected: Export (n)… copies them to a folder in an image format of your choice (videos are copied as MP4); Delete (n) sends them to the Recycle Bin; Clear selection.
  • The X on a thumbnail deletes it; Del deletes the selected items.

Naming captures

Give a capture a name with Rename… or F2 in the tray or the library. The name replaces the date under the thumbnail, becomes the document title and can be searched. Save as also names an unnamed capture. Leave the name empty to show the date again.

Backing up your settings

All your settings can be saved in a single .snapsettings file: the options, presets, themes and quick styles, favorites, tool styles, quick effects, stamp order and toolbar layout.

  • File › Export all settings… creates the file. Use it to move to a new computer or share a setup with your team.
  • File › Import all settings… reads it and asks whether to Replace (the file's settings take over) or Merge (keep yours and add what is missing, such as presets, themes, favorites and quick effects). A copy of your current settings is saved first, and both programs restart.
  • Automatic backup: once a week Snapnab saves a backup by itself and keeps the last 5. File › Open the backups folder shows them; import one to go back.
Captures are not in the settings file.To back up the captures themselves, copy the Library folder (see below).

Command line

Useful for scripts, launchers and shortcuts on the desktop or a stream deck.

Snapnab.exe [options]
  /hide            start in the notification area only
  /capture         capture an image with the current image preset
  /video           record video with the current video preset
  /preset "name"   run a preset by name
  /clipboard       capture the image on the clipboard
  /exit            close the running Snapnab
  /help            show this list

SnapnabEditor.exe [options] [file…]
  /Open "file"       open a file (.snap, image, .mp4)
  /NewFromClipboard  create an image from the clipboard
  /help              show this list

If Snapnab is already running, the command is passed to the running copy.

Troubleshooting and FAQ

Print Screen opens the Windows Snipping Tool instead of Snapnab.

Snapnab normally takes over the key automatically. If it does not, turn off Windows Settings › Accessibility › Keyboard › Use the Print screen key to open screen capture, then restart Snapnab, or choose another shortcut in File › Options….

A shortcut does nothing.

Check that Snapnab is running (its icon is in the notification area). Another program may own the combination; open File › Options… or the preset's Shortcut… to choose a different one.

Where did my capture go?

Every capture is in the Library: open the Editor and click Library. If Preview in Editor was off, the capture was only stored there (and sent to the other destinations).

The scrolling capture is cut or repeats parts.

Try Panoramic scrolling and include only the scrolling content in the frame. Scroll at a steady pace.

Grab Text says no OCR language is installed.

Add a language with its text recognition feature in Windows Settings › Time & language › Language.

My microphone is too quiet or too loud.

Open the volume panel on the recording panel and adjust the slider while watching the meter, or turn on Automatic microphone gain.

The webcam does not appear.

Allow camera access in Windows Settings › Privacy & security › Camera, close other programs that use the camera, and check that the camera is selected in the Webcam menu.

The recording does not start or the video fails.

Turn off Hardware encoding in Video options, check the free disk space, and try a lower frame rate. File › Open the logs folder contains details that help diagnose the problem.

Send by e-mail does nothing.

It needs a desktop e-mail program registered as the Windows default mail client. Otherwise, drag the thumbnail from the tray into your web mail.

Can I move my settings to another computer?

Yes: Export all settings on the old computer and import them on the new one. Copy the Library folder too if you want your captures.

The interface is in the wrong language.

Choose the language in File › Options… › Language. This manual is in English.
